    What changes when you switch?

    Run, year, universe and reference treatment select stored JNS and ANS results. The Journal Network Share (JNS) is a journal’s share of citation-network prestige within a universe; the Article Network Score (ANS) adjusts it for the journal’s share of eligible publications. Neither is recalculated by this page. Both follow the recursive citation-network method of Bergstrom, West and Wiseman (2008), computed from open OpenAlex data.

    For score year t, publications are articles and reviews from t−5 to t−1, and citations are those made in year t to these publications, excluding journal self-citations. Reference coverage is the share of these publications with at least one linked OpenAlex reference. It describes what OpenAlex records, not whether an article has a bibliography. By default the page shows Filtered scores, which only count publications with linked references; the setting “Include publications with no linked references” switches to Raw scores.

    A dash means a journal is in the universe but has no score. Journals outside the selected universe are listed last, with a link to the other universe. Zero is shown as a number.

    Which journals define a percentile?

    Start with all journals in the selected universe with a defined score. Apply the reference-coverage and publication-history requirements. Within each field, percentile = 100 × number of eligible journals with an equal or lower score ÷ number of eligible journals in that field.

    Keep journals whose field percentile is strictly above 100 minus the retained top percentage. Ties receive their highest shared percentile, so the retained share can exceed the requested percentage. The pool percentile then uses the same formula across all retained journals. With ANS selected, this is the ANS percentile on a 0–100 scale.

    Search, filters, visible columns, sorting and pages only change what you see. They never change the comparison group. Journals without a field form an explicit “Unclassified” group.
